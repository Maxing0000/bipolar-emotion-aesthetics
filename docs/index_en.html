📖 What is BEA?

Bipolar Emotion Aesthetics (BEA) is a formal aesthetics theory that proposes beauty arises from the combination of two opposing emotional poles, organized within recognizable order, and constrained within safe thresholds.

Every formal element (shape, color, texture, composition, light, sound, motion) occupies a position between two poles:

🟠 Pleasure Pole (P+)

Approach · Safety · Comfort
Round shapes, soft colors, smooth textures, gentle rhythm, symmetry, stability

🔵 Threat Pole (T−)

Arousal · Alertness · Tension
Sharp angles, high contrast, hard textures, abrupt changes, imbalance, coldness

Single polarity cannot produce advanced beauty: pure P+ becomes cloying and bland, pure T− becomes aggressive and repulsive. When both poles coexist within recognizable order, and the threat pole is constrained within the safety threshold, the arousal is "captured" as pleasure — creating the compound delight of "being attracted yet comforted."

📐 Core Methodology

Five Basic Concepts

  • Element — The smallest distinguishable formal unit (a line, a color, a texture, a sound)
  • Polarity — The P/T direction of an element, continuous and gradable (0–10)
  • Tension — Arousal intensity created by bipolar opposition, determines whether something is "engaging"
  • Order — Recognizable structure (symmetry, proportion, rhythm, hierarchy, resonance), digests tension into beauty
  • Threshold — The boundary where threat pole turns from pleasure to discomfort (instinctive/cognitive/cultural levels)

Tension × Order Beauty Map

Beauty stably appears in the High Tension × High Order Golden Zone. Improvement has only two directions: add order (for chaotic tension) or add tension (for orderly blandness).

W(T) Calculation

W(T) = Σ wᵢ · (tᵢ / 10) — where wᵢ is the dimension weight for the category, tᵢ is the threat intensity (0–10). Result falls between 0–1, corresponding to the paradigm spectrum position.
